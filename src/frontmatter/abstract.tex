\chapter*{ABSTRACT}
\phantomsection
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{ABSTRACT}

This report studies adaptive signal control at the Thapathali
intersection in Kathmandu. The problem is not only that traffic volume is
high; the heavier movement changes by time of day, so a fixed signal
cycle can keep serving one approach after its queue has reduced while
another approach continues to wait. The project therefore tests whether a
Deep Q-Network (DQN) controller can make better phase decisions from the
traffic state available in simulation.

The SUMO network was prepared from OpenStreetMap geometry and traffic
demand derived from five days of Origin-Destination (OD) and
vehicle-count data for Thapathali. At each 15-second decision step, the
agent receives a 21-dimensional state vector: PCU-weighted incoming
loads, outgoing loads, stopline waiting times, and the active signal
phase. The action space was kept deliberately small. The controller can
hold the current phase (STAY) or advance to the next phase (NEXT) in a
three-phase cycle, with yellow time before the change.

The reward uses a Max-Pressure term with an additional waiting-time
penalty. This was chosen to clear loaded approaches while still
penalising long waits on smaller movements.

After 300 training episodes, the DQN policy was tested against a fixed
60-second plan on Day 5 demand. Average waiting time decreased by
22.90\%, average queue length by 30.06\%, and throughput efficiency
improved by 32.65\%.

The strongest improvements occurred in low-to-moderate demand, where
fixed timing often wastes green time. Under peak saturation, the gain was
smaller. This shows that signal control can improve allocation, but it
cannot remove the capacity limit of the intersection itself.
