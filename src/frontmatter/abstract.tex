\chapter*{ABSTRACT}
\phantomsection
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{ABSTRACT}

Traffic congestion at the Thapathali intersection in Kathmandu causes significant delays, fuel waste, and commuter stress. Existing fixed-time signal control, reliant on pre-set cycle lengths and manual judgment, fails to adapt to the intersection's dynamic and asymmetric demand patterns, particularly during peak hours. This project proposes a Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL)-based adaptive traffic signal control system using the Deep Q-Network (DQN) algorithm, trained within a SUMO microscopic traffic simulation environment constructed from OpenStreetMap data and calibrated against five days of field-collected Origin-Destination (OD) and vehicle count data from the Thapathali intersection. The DQN agent represents intersection state as a 21-dimensional vector encoding PCU-weighted vehicle loads on incoming and outgoing lanes, maximum waiting times at stoplines, and the currently active signal phase. It selects between two actions, STAY and NEXT, over a cyclic sequence of three signal phases at 15-second decision intervals, guided by a Max-Pressure based reward function that balances throughput and wait-time equity. The agent was trained over 300 episodes using experience replay and an epsilon-greedy exploration strategy. Comparative evaluation against a fixed 60-second signal plan on Day 5 traffic demand demonstrates consistent improvement across all 17 hours of operation: average waiting time was reduced by 22.90\%, average queue length by 30.06\%, and throughput efficiency (vehicles per wait-second) improved by 32.65\%. The system performs best during low-to-moderate demand periods, where fixed-time control is structurally wasteful, and shows more modest gains during peak saturation where infrastructure capacity is the binding constraint. These results establish a credible simulation-based foundation for adaptive signal control at the Thapathali intersection, with identified pathways toward real-world deployment including multi-intersection coordination and integration of pedestrian and public transport flows.
